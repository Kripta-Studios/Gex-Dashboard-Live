% ABSTRACT

\begin{abstract}
We present the complete architecture of an automated intraday trading system for zero-days-to-expiration (0DTE) index options on the S\&P~500 (SPX) and NASDAQ-100 (QQQ). The system operates as a two-stage pipeline: (1)~a \emph{directional classifier} based on an ensemble of Gradient Boosted Trees (GBT) trained via anchored walk-forward validation emits a probabilistic signal $\hat{y} \in \{\textsc{short}, \textsc{hold}, \textsc{long}\}$; (2)~a \emph{Proximal Policy Optimization} (PPO) agent consumes this signal together with a 175-dimensional state vector to execute option strike selection, entry timing, and exit management with real-time options pricing.

This document provides the mathematical foundations, design rationale, training methodology, reward engineering, and empirical results that justify the architectural evolution from a legacy Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to the current GBT+RL framework. The migration resolved a fundamental over-parameterization failure, recovering a true out-of-sample directional edge of $+10.7\%$ above base rate and enabling profitable execution with a profit factor exceeding $1.5$ across walk-forward windows spanning 897 trading days.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Objective}
The primary objective is to construct a system that, at each 5-minute bar $t$ during U.S.\ equity market hours (09:30--16:00~ET), produces:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A \textbf{directional classification} $\hat{y}_t \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ corresponding to $\{\textsc{short}, \textsc{hold}, \textsc{long}\}$ with an associated confidence $c_t = \max_{k} p_k(t) \in [0, 1]$.
    \item Given a non-\textsc{hold} signal with $c_t \geq c_{\min}$, an \textbf{execution policy} $\pi_\theta$ that selects the option strike (via delta bucket), times the entry within a sniper observation window, and manages the position until exit.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Market Microstructure}
The system operates on 0DTE SPX options—contracts whose expiration falls on the current trading day. These instruments exhibit:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Extreme theta decay:} time value vanishes entirely by 16:00~ET, creating an exponentially accelerating cost of holding.
    \item \textbf{High gamma exposure:} near-expiration options exhibit convex payoffs, amplifying directional moves.
    \item \textbf{Wide bid-ask spreads:} deeply OTM strikes can exhibit spreads of $6\%$ of mid price, while ITM spreads compress to $\sim1\%$.
\end{itemize}

We model transaction costs via a delta-regime half-spread estimate:
\begin{equation}
    \text{HalfSpread}(|\Delta|) =
    \begin{cases}
        0.06 \cdot P_{\text{mid}} & \text{if } |\Delta| < 0.30 \quad (\text{OTM}) \\
        0.03 \cdot P_{\text{mid}} & \text{if } 0.30 \leq |\Delta| \leq 0.60 \quad (\text{ATM}) \\
        0.01 \cdot P_{\text{mid}} & \text{if } |\Delta| > 0.60 \quad (\text{ITM})
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:spread}
\end{equation}

\section{Stage 1: Directional Classifier (GBT Ensemble)}
\label{sec:gbt}

\subsection{Architectural Migration: MLP $\to$ GBT}
\label{subsec:migration}

The legacy architecture employed a PyTorch-based Multi-Layer Perceptron with approximately 150,000 trainable parameters. Walk-forward validation revealed a catastrophic failure mode: the model achieved $0\%$ precision and recall for out-of-sample directional trades at all confidence thresholds above $0.50$.

\subsubsection{Root Cause: Over-Parameterization}
The fundamental mismatch lay in the model capacity relative to sample size. The training dataset contained approximately $138{,}000$ individual 5-minute bars, but directional signals (LONG or SHORT) accounted for merely $13\%$ of observations. After class balancing, the effective training set per walk-forward window was approximately $14{,}000$ samples.

\begin{proposition}[Sample-to-Parameter Ratio]
For a neural network with $P$ parameters trained on $N$ effective samples in a noisy financial prediction setting with signal-to-noise ratio $\text{SNR} \ll 1$, the generalization error scales as:
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{\text{gen}} \propto \sqrt{\frac{P}{N}} \cdot \frac{1}{\text{SNR}}
\end{equation}
With $P = 150{,}000$ and $N = 14{,}000$, the ratio $P/N \approx 10.7$ guarantees catastrophic overfitting under the low-SNR financial regime.
\end{proposition}

The MLP learned training data perfectly ($c_{\text{train}} = 1.00$) but produced out-of-sample confidence rarely exceeding $0.45$, well below the actionable threshold of $0.55$.

\subsubsection{Diagnostic Test}
To isolate the failure locus (features vs.\ architecture), a LightGBM model was trained on $85\%$ of the temporal timeline and tested strictly out-of-sample on the remaining $15\%$:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Task} & \textbf{GBT Accuracy} & \textbf{Base Rate} & \textbf{Edge} \\
\midrule
Multiclass (Short/Hold/Long) & 91.0\% & 90.2\% & +0.8\% \\
Directional (Short vs Long) & 72.2\% & 61.5\% & \textbf{+10.7\%} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{LightGBM diagnostic test—proof that the 163 features contain a robust directional signal that the MLP was incapable of extracting.}
\label{tab:diagnostic}
\end{table}

This established that the features contain statistically significant directional information, and the failure was architectural, not informational.

\subsection{Why Gradient Boosted Trees}
Gradient Boosted Trees represent the empirically dominant architecture for tabular data with $N < 10^6$ \cite{grinsztajn2022}. Key advantages:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Implicit feature selection:} tree splits naturally ignore uninformative features, providing built-in regularization.
    \item \textbf{Scale invariance:} unlike neural networks, GBTs are invariant to monotone feature transformations, eliminating the sensitivity to normalization.
    \item \textbf{Data efficiency:} GBTs extract robust non-linear interactions from orders of magnitude fewer samples than deep neural networks.
    \item \textbf{No gradient vanishing:} each tree fits the residual directly, avoiding the vanishing gradient problem in deep networks.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{LightGBM Configuration}
\label{subsec:lgbm_config}

Each individual model uses the following hyperparameter configuration:

\label{tab:lgbm_params}
\end{table}

The GBT loss function is the standard multiclass log-loss (cross-entropy):
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{GBT}} = -\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{k=0}^{2} y_{ik} \log \hat{p}_{ik}
    \label{eq:gbt_loss}
\end{equation}
where $y_{ik} \in \{0, 1\}$ is the one-hot target and $\hat{p}_{ik}$ is the softmax probability for class $k$.

\subsection{Feature Normalization}
Despite GBT's inherent scale invariance, a feature normalizer computes per-column statistics for downstream compatibility with the RL agent's neural network backbone:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{x}_j = \frac{x_j - \mu_j}{\sigma_j + \epsilon}, \quad \epsilon = 10^{-8}
    \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
The normalizer is fit on the \textbf{full training set} (before class balancing) to capture the true feature distribution, then applied to all downstream consumers.

\section{Walk-Forward Training Pipeline}
\label{sec:walkforward}

\subsection{Temporal Splitting}
Financial time series exhibit non-stationarity and autocorrelation, making random train/test splits invalid. We employ \textbf{anchored walk-forward validation}:

\begin{definition}[Walk-Forward Window]
Given a dataset spanning $T$ months, with training window $W_{\text{train}}$ and test window $W_{\text{test}}$, the $i$-th split is defined as:
\begin{align}
    \text{Train}_i &= [t_{i-1}, \; t_{i-1} + W_{\text{train}}) \\
    \text{Test}_i  &= [t_{i-1} + W_{\text{train}}, \; t_{i-1} + W_{\text{train}} + W_{\text{test}})
\end{align}
with step size $\Delta = 1$ month. This generates $\lfloor (T - W_{\text{train}}) / \Delta \rfloor$ windows.
\end{definition}

In production, $W_{\text{train}} = 9$ months and $W_{\text{test}} = 1$ month. For a dataset spanning August 2022 to February 2026 ($\sim42$ months), this produces approximately 33 walk-forward windows.

\subsection{Class Balancing}
\label{subsec:balancing}
Financial labeling produces inherently unbalanced classes, with HOLD typically comprising $87\%$ of observations. We apply the following balancing algorithm:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Let $n_L = |\{i : y_i = \text{LONG}\}|$ and $n_S = |\{i : y_i = \text{SHORT}\}|$.
    \item Set the minority count $n_{\min} = \max(\min(n_L, n_S), \; 30)$.
    \item Balance directional classes: $n_L' = n_S' = n_{\min}$ (strict 1:1 ratio).
    \item Cap HOLD samples: $n_H' = \min(n_H, \; 2 \cdot (n_L' + n_S'))$.
    \item Reject the window if $n_L' < 100$ or $n_S' < 100$.
\end{enumerate}

This ensures zero directional bias and prevents label-sparse windows from producing unreliable models.

\subsection{Calibration}
\label{subsec:calibration}
Each model's decision thresholds are calibrated on a \emph{calibration set} (the first 10 trading days of the test period). This prevents threshold optimization from contaminating the out-of-sample evaluation.

The trade calibration function converts raw class probabilities $\bm{p} = (p_{\text{S}}, p_{\text{H}}, p_{\text{L}})$ into a discrete prediction:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y} =
    \begin{cases}
        \text{LONG}  & \text{if } p_{\text{L}} \geq \tau_L \;\wedge\; p_{\text{L}} \geq p_{\text{S}} \;\wedge\; p_{\text{L}} \geq p_{\text{H}} + m \\
        \text{SHORT} & \text{if } p_{\text{S}} \geq \tau_S \;\wedge\; p_{\text{S}} > p_{\text{L}} \;\wedge\; p_{\text{S}} \geq p_{\text{H}} + m \\
        \text{HOLD}  & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_L, \tau_S \in [0.50, 0.80]$ are per-class confidence thresholds and $m \in \{0.00, 0.05, 0.10\}$ is the hold margin. These are grid-searched on the calibration set to maximize profit factor subject to a minimum trade count constraint ($\geq 10$).

\subsection{Window Selection and Ensemble Construction}
\label{subsec:ensemble}

Not all walk-forward windows produce viable models. A multi-stage filter removes degenerate windows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Collapse detection:} reject if $>90\%$ of directional predictions are one-sided.
    \item \textbf{Minimum trades:} reject if calibrated predictions produce fewer than $\max(10, |\text{cal}|/50)$ trades.
    \item \textbf{Profit factor floor:} reject if PF $< 0.30$ with $\geq 30$ trades.
\end{enumerate}

Surviving windows are ranked by a \textbf{recency-weighted score} that penalizes older windows:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Score}(w_i) = \overline{\text{PF}}_i \cdot \left(\frac{i}{W_{\text{total}}}\right)^2
    \label{eq:rank_score}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\text{PF}}_i$ is the average profit factor across the window's ensemble constituents and $i / W_{\text{total}}$ is the recency fraction. The quadratic exponent heavily penalizes models trained on data more than 12 months old.

The top $N = 10$ windows contribute their models to the production ensemble. Each window trains $K = 5$ models with different random seeds, yielding up to $N \times K = 50$ constituent models. The ensemble prediction is the arithmetic mean of individual model probabilities:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\bm{p}}_{\text{ensemble}}(\bx) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \hat{\bm{p}}_m(\bx)
    \label{eq:ensemble}
\end{equation}
where $M$ is the total number of accepted models across all selected windows.

\subsection{Temporal Sample Weighting}
To address non-stationarity, exponential decay weights are applied to training samples:
\begin{equation}
    w_i = \exp\left(-\frac{t_{\max} - t_i}{\tau_{\text{decay}}}\right), \quad \tau_{\text{decay}} = 30 \text{ days}
    \label{eq:temporal_weights}
\end{equation}
This ensures that recent market regimes contribute disproportionately to the gradient during boosting.

\section{Target Label Engineering}
\label{sec:labeling}

\subsection{Asymmetric Profit/Stop Labeling}
Given the well-documented negative skewness of equity returns, we employ asymmetric profit targets:

\begin{definition}[Directional Label]
At bar $t$ with spot price $S_t$, scanning forward up to $L = 180$ minutes:
\begin{equation}
    y_t =
    \begin{cases}
        \textsc{long}  & \text{if } \exists \, t' \in [t+1, t+L] : S_{t'} \geq S_t (1 + \alpha_L) \;\wedge\; \min_{\tau \in [t, t']} S_\tau > S_t(1 - \sigma) \\
        \textsc{short} & \text{if } \exists \, t' \in [t+1, t+L] : S_{t'} \leq S_t (1 - \alpha_S) \;\wedge\; \max_{\tau \in [t, t']} S_\tau < S_t(1 + \sigma) \\
        \textsc{hold}  & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_L = 1.0\%$ (long profit target), $\alpha_S = 0.5\%$ (short profit target), $\sigma = 0.3\%$ (stop loss), and $L = 180$ minutes (lookahead window).
\end{definition}

The asymmetry ($\alpha_L > \alpha_S$) reflects the empirically observed prevalence of large upside moves in equity indices, ensuring a balanced label distribution under trending market conditions.

\subsection{Maximum Move Computation}
For each labeled bar $t$, the \textbf{maximum favorable move} within the lookahead window is computed:
\begin{equation}
    M_t = \max_{\tau \in [t+1, t+L]} \; \frac{|S_\tau - S_t|}{S_t}
    \label{eq:max_move}
\end{equation}
This quantity is propagated to the RL reward function as \texttt{max\_move}, enabling the under-capture penalty described in Section~\ref{subsec:terminal_reward}.

\section{Stage 2: RL Execution Agent (PPO)}
\label{sec:rl}

\subsection{Motivation}
The directional classifier provides a probabilistic signal; the RL agent transforms this signal into profitable option trades. The agent must learn three interrelated skills:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strike selection:} choosing the option delta (moneyness) that balances premium cost against directional leverage.
    \item \textbf{Entry timing:} waiting within a 15-minute observation window for a favorable entry price (the ``sniper'' mode).
    \item \textbf{Exit management:} deciding each minute whether to hold or exit the position, balancing unrealized gains against theta decay and adverse moves.
\end{enumerate}

\label{subsec:state}
The agent observes a state vector $\bs_t \in \R^{175}$ composed of four groups:

\midrule
\textbf{Total} & \textbf{175} & \\
\bottomrule

The position state components are:
\begin{align}

    q_4 &= \theta_t / P_0 \quad \text{(theta normalized by entry premium)} \label{eq:q4} \\
    q_5 &= \text{clip}(\text{IV}_t / 0.15, \; 0.5, \; 3.0) \label{eq:q5} \\
    q_6 &= \text{clip}(\text{MAE}, -1.0, 0.0) \label{eq:q6}
\end{align}

The agent operates in multiple \textbf{action phases}, each with a distinct discrete action space:

The 7 delta buckets map directly to target option deltas:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_k \in \{0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50, 0.60, 0.70\}
    \label{eq:delta_buckets}
\end{equation}
corresponding to labels: deep OTM, OTM far, OTM near, OTM light, ATM, ITM light, ITM.


\subsection{Network Architecture}
\label{subsec:network}

The PPO agent uses a \textbf{shared-backbone, multi-head} architecture:

\begin{equation}
    \bs \in \R^{175} \xrightarrow{\text{Backbone}} \bm{h} \in \R^{128} \xrightarrow{\text{Heads}}
    \begin{cases}
        \pi_{\text{strike}}(\cdot|\bs) & \in \Delta^6 \\
        \pi_{\text{sniper}}(\cdot|\bs) & \in \Delta^7 \\
        \pi_{\text{exit}}(\cdot|\bs)   & \in \Delta^1 \\
        f(\bs) \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2) & \text{(sizing)} \\
        V(\bs) & \in \R \;\text{(critic)}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:architecture}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Backbone}
The shared backbone processes the 175-dimensional state through three fully-connected blocks:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Backbone}: \R^{175} \xrightarrow{W_1} \R^{256} \xrightarrow{\text{LN}} \xrightarrow{\text{GELU}} \xrightarrow{\text{Drop}(0.1)} \R^{256} \xrightarrow{W_2} \cdots \xrightarrow{W_3} \R^{128}
    \label{eq:backbone}
\end{equation}
Each block applies: Linear $\to$ LayerNorm $\to$ GELU $\to$ Dropout$(0.1)$.

\subsubsection{Action Heads}
Each head is a two-layer MLP: $\R^{128} \to \R^{64} \to \R^{|\mathcal{A}|}$, with GELU activation. The sizing head outputs $(\mu, \log\sigma)$ parameterizing a Gaussian mapped through a sigmoid squash:
\begin{align}
    z &\sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2) \\
    f &= \text{sigmoid}(z) \in (0, 1) \\
    \log \pi(f | \bs) &= \log \mathcal{N}(z; \mu, \sigma^2) - \log \big|f(1 - f)\big|
    \label{eq:sizing}
\end{align}
The Jacobian correction ensures valid probability density under the change of variables.

\subsubsection{Weight Initialization}
All linear layers are initialized with orthogonal weights (gain $= 0.5$) and zero biases, following established practice for PPO to prevent premature policy collapse.

The total parameter count is approximately $\mathbf{187{,}348}$.

\subsection{PPO Training}
\label{subsec:ppo}

The agent is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization with clipped surrogate objective:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{PPO}} = -\E_t \left[\min\left(r_t(\theta) \hat{A}_t, \; \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon) \hat{A}_t\right)\right]
    \label{eq:ppo}
\end{equation}
where $r_t(\theta) = \pi_\theta(a_t | s_t) / \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t | s_t)$ is the probability ratio and $\hat{A}_t$ is the Generalized Advantage Estimate (GAE):
\begin{equation}
    \hat{A}_t = \sum_{l=0}^{\infty} (\gamma \lambda)^l \delta_{t+l}, \quad \delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)
    \label{eq:gae}
\end{equation}

The total loss combines policy, value, and entropy terms:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{PPO}} + c_v \cdot \mathcal{L}_V - c_H \cdot H[\pi_\theta]
    \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}

Training proceeds in three phases with decreasing constraints:

\section{Reward Function Design}
\label{sec:rewards}

The reward function is the most critical design decision in the RL agent. We employ a \textbf{hybrid dense+sparse} reward structure:

\subsection{Step Reward (Dense)}
At each minute $t$ while holding a position, the agent receives:
\begin{equation}
    r_t^{\text{step}} = 0.3 \cdot \Delta_t^{\text{PnL}} - 0.02 \cdot |\Theta_t^{\text{pen}}| - 0.05 \cdot |D_t^{\text{pen}}|
    \label{eq:step_reward}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Delta_t^{\text{PnL}} = \text{PnL}_t - \text{PnL}_{t-1}$ is the instantaneous P\&L change.
    \item $\Theta_t^{\text{pen}}$ is the theta penalty, exponentially weighted by minutes to close:
    \begin{equation}
        \Theta_t^{\text{pen}} =
        \begin{cases}
            0 & \text{if PnL}_t > 10\% \\
            \frac{\theta_t}{P_0} \cdot \left(1 + 2e^{-m_t/60}\right) & \text{otherwise}
        \end{cases}
        \label{eq:theta}
    \end{equation}
    where $m_t$ is minutes to market close and the exponential term doubles the penalty in the last hour.
    \item $D_t^{\text{pen}}$ is the drawdown penalty, active only on new MAE extensions:
    \begin{equation}
        D_t^{\text{pen}} =
        \begin{cases}
            0.2 \cdot (\text{PnL}_t - \text{MAE}_t) & \text{if PnL}_t < \text{MAE}_t \;\wedge\; \text{PnL}_t < 0 \\
            0 & \text{otherwise}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

Step rewards are intentionally small ($|r_t^{\text{step}}| \ll 1$) to prevent the dense signal from overwhelming the sparse terminal reward.

\subsection{Sniper Step Reward}
During the PRE\_ENTRY phase, step rewards are \textbf{identically zero}:
\begin{equation}
    r_t^{\text{sniper}} = 0.0, \quad \forall t \in [t_{\text{signal}}, t_{\text{signal}} + 15]
\end{equation}
The timing signal comes entirely from the terminal reward contrast: better entries produce higher terminal PnL.

\subsection{Terminal Reward (Sparse)}
\label{subsec:terminal_reward}
Upon position exit, the agent receives a multi-component terminal reward:

\begin{equation}
    R^{\text{term}} = \text{clip}\bigg[\big(R^{\text{PnL}} + R^{\text{patience}} + R^{\text{stop}} + R^{\text{strike}} + R^{\text{exit}} + R^{\text{capture}} + R^{\text{entry}}\big) \cdot c_{\text{scale}}, \; -3, \; +5\bigg]
    \label{eq:terminal}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\text{scale}} = 0.5 + c_t$ and $c_t$ is the GBT confidence at signal time. Each component:

\subsubsection{1. Core P\&L Reward (Asymmetric)}
\begin{equation}
    R^{\text{PnL}} =
    \begin{cases}
        2.0 \cdot \tilde{P} & \text{if } \tilde{P} \geq 0 \\
        1.5 \cdot \tilde{P} & \text{if } \tilde{P} < 0
    \end{cases}
    , \quad \tilde{P} = P_{\text{final}} \cdot \frac{0.15}{\max(\text{IV}_0, 0.05)}
    \label{eq:pnl_reward}
\end{equation}
The volatility scaling normalizes P\&L to a reference IV of 15\%, ensuring that the agent is equally rewarded for a +30\% gain in a low-vol environment as a +30\% gain in a high-vol environment. The asymmetric multipliers ($2.0$ vs $1.5$) create a positive expected reward for break-even policies, nudging the agent toward profitable trades.

\subsubsection{2. Patience Bonus}
Replaces the legacy speed-based bonus to prevent premature exits:
\begin{equation}
    R^{\text{patience}} =
    \begin{cases}
        0.5 + 0.3 \cdot P_{\text{final}} & \text{if } P_{\text{final}} \geq 100\% \\
        0.3 \cdot P_{\text{final}} \cdot (0.3 + 0.7 \cdot \text{patience}) & \text{if } 30\% \leq P_{\text{final}} < 100\% \\
        0.1 \cdot P_{\text{final}} & \text{if } 0 < P_{\text{final}} < 30\% \\
        0 & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:patience}
\end{equation}
where $\text{patience} = \min(t_{\text{held}} / 90, \; 1.0)$ saturates at 90 minutes. This construction rewards holding through large winners—the agent receives more for a +80\% gain held for 60 minutes than a +30\% gain taken after 10 minutes.

\subsubsection{3. Hard Stop Penalty}
\begin{equation}
    R^{\text{stop}} =
    \begin{cases}
        -1.0 & \text{if exit type = hard stop loss} \\
        0 & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{4. Strike Quality Bonus}
Corrected from a prior implementation bug where the ITM bonus was silently overwritten:
\begin{equation}
    R^{\text{strike}} =
    \begin{cases}
        +0.50 & \text{if } |\Delta_0| \geq 0.50 \;\wedge\; P_{\text{final}} > 0 \quad (\text{ITM winner}) \\
        +0.10 & \text{if } 0.25 \leq |\Delta_0| \leq 0.45 \;\wedge\; P_{\text{final}} > 0 \quad (\text{OTM sweet-spot}) \\
        +0.05 & \text{if } |\Delta_0| \geq 0.50 \;\wedge\; P_{\text{final}} \leq 0 \quad (\text{ITM exploration}) \\
        0 & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:strike}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{5. Early Exit Penalty}
Penalizes panic exits on losing positions (but not quick profitable escapes):
\begin{equation}
    R^{\text{exit}} =
    \begin{cases}
        -0.30 & \text{if agent exit} \;\wedge\; t_{\text{held}} < 0.10 \cdot T_{\max} \;\wedge\; P_{\text{final}} \leq 0 \\
        0 & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{6. Under-Capture Penalty}
Penalizes the agent for exiting a winning trade having captured less than 30\% of the available move:
\begin{equation}
    R^{\text{capture}} =
    \begin{cases}
        -0.2 \cdot (1 - \rho) & \text{if } M_t > 0 \;\wedge\; P_{\text{final}} > 0 \;\wedge\; \rho < 0.30 \\
        0 & \text{otherwise}
    \end{cases}
    , \quad \rho = \frac{P_{\text{final}}}{M_t}
    \label{eq:capture}
\end{equation}
where $M_t$ is the maximum favorable move from Equation~(\ref{eq:max_move}).

\subsubsection{7. Entry Timing Bonus}
Rewards favorable price improvement during the sniper window:
\begin{equation}
    R^{\text{entry}} = \min(5 \cdot \Delta_{\text{price}}, \; 0.3), \quad \Delta_{\text{price}} > 0
    \label{eq:entry}
\end{equation}

\section{RL Environment: SPXOptionsEnv}
\label{sec:environment}

\subsection{Episode Structure}
Each episode represents a single trade lifecycle triggered by a GBT directional signal:

\begin{equation}
    \underbrace{\text{Signal}}_{\text{GBT}} \;\to\; \underbrace{\text{PRE\_ENTRY}}_{\substack{\text{15-min sniper} \\ \text{observation}}} \;\to\; \underbrace{\text{IN\_POSITION}}_{\substack{\text{Hold/Exit} \\ \text{decisions}}} \;\to\; \underbrace{\text{CLOSED}}_{\text{Terminal reward}}
    \label{eq:episode}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Options Pricing}
The environment uses \textbf{real ThetaData options pricing} for mark-to-market at each timestep. For timestamps without available quotes, a delta-gamma approximation is used:
\begin{equation}
    P_t \approx P_0 + \Delta \cdot (S_t - S_0) + \tfrac{1}{2} \Gamma \cdot (S_t - S_0)^2
    \label{eq:delta_gamma}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stratified Sampling}
To ensure balanced training across market regimes, episodes are stratified by VIX regime and time of day. The sampling strata prevent the agent from over-fitting to low-volatility, mid-day conditions.

\section{System Integration}
\label{sec:integration}

\subsection{GBT–RL Interface}
A key engineering requirement was preserving pipeline compatibility during the MLP$\to$GBT migration. The \texttt{GBTEnsemble} compatibility layer wraps the native LightGBM \texttt{predict\_proba()} method and exposes a PyTorch \texttt{\_\_call\_\_} signature:

\begin{equation}
    \texttt{GBTEnsemble}(\bx) \to (\underbrace{\bm{p}}_{\text{logits} \in \R^3}, \; \underbrace{(60, 0)}_{\text{dummy time pred}})
\end{equation}

This ensures that the RL agent's \texttt{IntegratedTradingSystem} and the PPO environment remain completely agnostic to the underlying mathematical engine. The probabilities are passed directly as ``logits'' (not log-transformed), since \texttt{softmax}($\bm{p}$) where $\sum p_k = 1$ introduces minimal distortion compared to \texttt{softmax}($\log \bm{p}$).

\subsection{Data Pipeline}
\begin{equation}
    \underbrace{\texttt{collect\_data}}_{\text{ThetaData API}} \;\to\; \underbrace{\texttt{train\_walkforward}}_{\text{GBT Ensemble}} \;\to\; \underbrace{\texttt{generate\_episodes}}_{\text{Episode Index}} \;\to\; \underbrace{\texttt{preprocess}}_{\text{Options Cache}} \;\to\; \underbrace{\texttt{rl.training}}_{\text{PPO Agent}}
\end{equation}

\section{Backtesting Framework}
\label{sec:backtest}

\subsection{Dual Simulation}
The backtesting system runs two parallel simulations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{GBT-Only (Synthetic Futures):} directional entry at spot price, exit at fixed target/stop, using OHLC-intrabar detection.
    \item \textbf{GBT+RL (Real Options):} RL agent selects strike from the real options chain, manages entry timing and exit using actual ThetaData greeks pricing.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Position Sizing}
Position size is a function of model confidence and account balance:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Risk}\% = 10\% + \frac{c_t - 0.55}{0.45} \times 40\%, \quad c_t \in [0.55, 1.00]
    \label{eq:sizing}
\end{equation}
This maps minimum confidence ($0.55$) to 10\% risk and maximum confidence ($1.00$) to 50\% risk.

\subsection{Drawdown Mitigation}
After 3 consecutive losses, the system enters ``drawdown mode'':
\begin{itemize}
    \item Position size is halved: $n_{\text{contracts}} \gets \max(1, \lfloor n/2 \rfloor)$.
    \item Drawdown mode exits when balance recovers to the pre-drawdown watermark.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The migration from a Multi-Layer Perceptron to Gradient Boosted Trees represents a pivotal alignment of model architecture with data structure. By deploying a model inherently suited for mid-sized tabular data with low signal-to-noise ratios, we successfully extracted the latent predictive signal within our 163-dimensional options feature space.

The two-stage GBT+RL architecture cleanly separates the directional classification problem (``which way?'') from the execution problem (``how to trade it?''), allowing each component to be optimized and validated independently. The walk-forward training methodology ensures no temporal leakage, while the multi-component reward function encourages the RL agent to develop patient, high-conviction trading behavior rather than the premature exit patterns observed under the legacy reward structure.

Key empirical results from the 897-day out-of-sample backtest (Table~\ref{tab:backtest_results}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GBT directional accuracy:} $72.2\%$ OOS on Short vs Long ($+10.7\%$ over base rate), with a production ensemble of 45 models drawn from the top walk-forward windows.
    \item \textbf{High-confidence precision:} $>90\%$ at $c_t \geq 0.65$.
    \item \textbf{GBT-only profit factor:} 4.95 across 1,380 trades at 67.3\% win rate.
    \item \textbf{GBT+RL profit factor:} \textbf{6.12} ($+23.8\%$ improvement), driven by a W/L size ratio of 2.69 vs.\ 1.88 in the GBT-only baseline.
    \item \textbf{RL agent:} 187,604-parameter PPO network; 80.4\% of exits are agent-initiated; average winning hold time reduced from 88 to 23 minutes.
    \item \textbf{Strike selection:} Near-uniform distribution across seven delta buckets confirms no degenerate policy collapse; ITM strikes achieve 68.9\% win rate.
\end{itemize}
